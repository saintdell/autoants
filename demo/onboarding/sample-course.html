Thermostat

What it does
Tells the system when to heat, cool or run the fan, through the thin cable to the furnace.
What fails
Dead batteries, wrong mode or setpoint, loose wires, a failed unit.
What you look at
Mode, setpoint, fan setting, and whether the display has power. Many no-cooling calls end here.

Air filter

What it does
Cleans the air before it reaches the blower and the coil.
What fails
Clogged: low airflow, a frozen coil, high bills, a furnace that overheats and shuts down.
What you look at
Pull it: how dirty is it? Is the airflow arrow pointing the right way? Is it the right size for the slot? Shop rule: members get a new filter from the van; for everyone else, show the customer and ask before you replace it.

Gas furnace

What it does
Heats with gas burners in winter. In summer its blower moves the cooled air through the ducts.
What fails
Igniter, flame sensor, safety switches, blower motor. A status light on the door often blinks a code.
What you look at
Read the status light against the code chart on the door (photo the chart and the blinking light into the job). Check the door switch and filter. Combustion and venting work is for trained techs.

Evaporator coil

What it does
Sits on top of the furnace and takes heat out of the air. Water forms on it, which is why it needs a drain.
What fails
Dirt on the coil, freezing (usually from low airflow or a refrigerant problem), a clogged drain pan.
What you look at
Ice on the coil or the suction line? Note it and call; don't chip ice. Check the filter first.

Condensate drain

What it does
Carries the water from the coil to a drain.
What fails
Algae and sludge clog it; water backs up into the pan or ceiling.
What you look at
Water standing in the tee or the pan? Clearing the line is the way Ray shows you on your first ride-along: wet-vac at the outside end, then flush from the tee.

Float switch

What it does
Shuts cooling off when the drain backs up, before water overflows.
What fails
Trips when the drain clogs (that's its job). Sometimes sticks.
What you look at
A tripped float switch looks like 'no cooling'. Clear the drain per your shop's procedure; the switch resets when the water drops.

Gas shutoff and drip leg

What it does
The valve shuts gas off to the furnace. The capped pipe below the tee (the drip leg, or sediment trap) catches debris before it reaches the furnace.
What fails
Leaks at fittings. Never use a flame to look for one.
What you look at
Know where the shutoff is and which way is off on this valve before you need it. Smell gas: stop and follow the gas steps in Safety stops.

Line set

What it does
Two copper lines between the outdoor unit and the indoor coil: the larger, insulated suction line and the smaller liquid line.
What fails
Torn or missing insulation, kinks, rub-through where a line touches something, leaks (often an oily spot at a joint).
What you look at
Is the insulation intact? Any kinks or rubbing? Ice or heavy sweat on the suction line is worth noting and calling in. Don't open anything (608).

Service valves

What it does
Where the line set connects to the outdoor unit. The capped ports are where a certified tech connects gauges.
What fails
Leaking caps or cores, oil stains at the joints.
What you look at
Look, don't open. Under EPA Section 608, only certified techs open the refrigerant circuit, and that includes connecting gauges. Shop rule: only Ray, or a certified tech Ray names, opens a service port at this shop.

Outdoor unit (condenser)

What it does
Moves the heat taken out of the house to the outdoors. Inside the cabinet: the compressor, the outdoor coil and the fan on top.
What fails
Capacitor, contactor, fan motor, a coil packed with dirt or grass clippings, debris in the fan.
What you look at
Is the fan turning when the system calls for cooling? Is the coil clean and the area around the unit clear? Listen before you touch: power safety first.

Run capacitor

What it does
Stores and releases charge to help the compressor and fan motor start and run.
What fails
A weak or failed capacitor is a common reason the fan or compressor won't start. Look for a bulged top or leaking oil.
What you look at
Look only, until the power is off, locked out and verified dead, and the capacitor is discharged and verified the way your shop trains you. A capacitor can hold a charge after the power is off.

Contactor

What it does
The switch that sends power to the compressor and outdoor fan when the thermostat calls for cooling.
What fails
Burned or pitted contacts, a failed coil, insects caught between the contacts.
What you look at
With power off and verified: look for burn marks, pitting and insects. Meter tests only as you've been trained.

Outdoor disconnect

What it does
A shutoff near the outdoor unit so it can be de-energized where you're working. The flexible whip carries power to the unit.
What fails
Corroded or loose connections, a pulled-out or missing block, a damaged whip.
What you look at
Off and locked out before you open the outdoor unit, together with the breaker. Then verify with your meter.

Breaker panel

What it does
Feeds the house circuits, including the outdoor unit and the furnace.
What fails
Tripped breakers. A breaker that trips again after a reset is telling you something.
What you look at
Find and label the AC and furnace breakers before you start. Opening the panel cover is not a first-day task. Shop rule: you don't take a panel cover off until Ray has signed you off on it.

Step ladder

What it does
How you reach attic units, high returns and ceiling access.
What fails
Bent rails, loose steps, unlocked spreaders. Most ladder falls are about setup, not the ladder.
What you look at
Inspect it, open it fully, lock the spreaders, follow the label on the highest step you may stand on, keep three points of contact.

Safety stops

Draft: waiting on a licensed tech's check for this shop

In this order, every time. The drawing shows each stop; tap a step to see it.

  1. Turn off the outdoor unit's breaker, and the furnace's if you'll work inside it. Lock and tag it per the lockout rule below (your lock, your tag, only you take it off).

  2. Pull or switch off the disconnect at the outdoor unit and lock it out if it takes a lock. Breaker and disconnect, both.

  3. Off is not proven until you test. Check your meter on a known live source, test the circuit you'll touch, then check the meter again. Shop rule: while you ride along, say each reading out loud to Ray.

  4. A capacitor can hold a charge after the power is off. Verify it's discharged before you touch the terminals, using the method and tool your shop trains you on.

  5. Only EPA Section 608-certified techs open the refrigerant circuit, including connecting gauges. Not certified? Leave the caps on, note what you see, and call it in.

  6. Know where the gas shutoff is. Smell gas: don't operate switches or anything that sparks, get people out, leave, and call from outside per Mike's gas rule (call the gas company's emergency line first, then Mike) and [the gas utility's emergency line] · not filled in yet. Carbon monoxide has no smell: wear a CO monitor if your shop issues one, and report soot, scorch marks or a loose vent pipe.

  7. Attics can get dangerously hot: drink water, take breaks, tell someone before you go up (text the job thread "going up" and "down"), and step only on joists or a walkway. Ladders: inspect, open fully, lock the spreaders, follow the label, three points of contact.

This shop's lockout rule

Every tech carries a lock and tags in the van door pocket. The tag says your name, the date and DO NOT TURN ON. Only the person who put the lock on takes it off. Before anyone touches a wire: check your meter on a known live source, test the circuit, check the meter again.

No cooling: the five checks before you call

Draft: waiting on a licensed tech's check for this shop

Most calls that feel stuck end at one of these. Go in order, and stop at anything on the stop-and-call list.

  1. Mode set to Cool? Setpoint below the room temperature? Is the display powered (a blank screen can be dead batteries)? Fan on Auto or On?

  2. Is either one off or tripped? Reset a tripped breaker only as Mike allows: one reset, with the customer's OK. If it trips again, stop: that's a call, not a retry.

  3. Pull the filter: clogged? Are the supply registers open and the return grille clear? Ice on the coil or the suction line means note it and call; don't chip it.

  4. Water standing in the drain tee or the pan? A tripped float switch shuts cooling off on purpose. Clear the drain per the way Ray shows you on your first ride-along: wet-vac at the outside end, then flush from the tee; the switch resets when the water drops.

  5. With the thermostat calling for cooling: is the fan turning and the compressor running? If not: power off, locked out and verified first. Then look at the contactor (burned, pitted, insects) and the capacitor (bulged top, leaking oil). Meter tests only as trained.

Stop and call: don't fix these yourself

  • Anything with refrigerant (EPA 608 only)
  • A gas smell
  • Burned, melted or scorched wiring
  • Anything outside your training
