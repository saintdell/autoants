We take one normal past week, plan it again under your own rules, and show it next to what you actually did. What didn't get better is said first.

101 delivery and install jobs, 5 days

4 days had a record of which crew ran each job, in what order; those are compared. Both columns are scored the same way, with the same crews, windows and install times.

Shift Desk · made-up restaurant group

24 people, 76 shifts, one week

The as-run column is the group's own time punches. The plan covers the same need under labor law and the rules the managers stated. People appear only as coded ids.

Said first, what didn't get better: labor cost is $50 higher on the plan, because it schedules enough people for the hours that ran short; weekend shifts are a little less even; the number of people over 40 hours didn't move.

What you send, what you get

  • You send one normal week from the system you already use (an export, or a photo of the board), and who ran what.
  • We watch your planner build one day, so the unwritten rules get written down.
  • You get a report: your week and the plan, day by day, every number labeled measured or modeled.

What it never does

Promises savings. Every plan number is modeled until it's measured on your own records, and your dispatcher, foreman or manager approves every plan.
