How to choose a roofer

  1. Check the license class, not only the number. Since January 1, 2026, anyone who advertises, bids or does work on the roof of a home, for a project of $7,500 or more, must hold LSLBC's Residential Roofing or Residential Construction classification (LSLBC memo). LSLBC's Roofing and Sheet Metal, Siding classification covers roof systems for buildings and structures (LSLBC). Look the company up on the board's own search, by number or by company name; the badges in this guide link to the board's record.
  2. Make sure the job is permitted and inspected. A 2025 state law (Act 239) requires permits and inspections for roof construction and reroofing on homes and commercial buildings, for projects begun on or after August 1, 2025, and another (Act 422) made it a violation for a contractor to start before the permit or skip the inspections (LSLBC memo). Ask who pulls the permit and when the inspection is.
  3. After a storm, verify before you sign or pay. After a major storm, LSLBC asks homeowners to hire only licensed contractors and to verify the contractor's license with the board before signing a contract or making a payment (LSLBC, Weathering the Storm).
  4. Get the scope in writing. Ask what's included: tear-off or overlay, decking repairs, flashing, gutters and haul-off. Compare the same things across quotes.

How this list was built

Who's listed. A company is listed when it is based in Orleans or Jefferson Parish, has a phone, and either holds an active LSLBC license in Residential Roofing or in Roofing and Sheet Metal, Siding, or appears in public business listings under a roofing-contractor category. For a company in a roofing listing, an LSLBC Residential Construction license also counts for the badge, because LSLBC names it alongside Residential Roofing for home roofs; a Home Improvement license doesn't. Duplicates (same phone) are listed once. We also need to confirm a real local base: an LSLBC match, or a street address in the parish shown on the company's own website next to the same phone, or on a City of New Orleans occupational license.

Who's left out. A license held under a person's own name; retail stores and supply houses; companies whose name says their main trade is something else (plumbing, painting, restoration and the like); companies found only on the LSLBC roster, in no roofing listing, whose name doesn't say roofing; and listings that look like call-routing services rather than a local company: an out-of-state phone number, three or more different names on near-identical phone numbers, a throwaway ".site" web address, or a name made of a city plus a service, unless it matches the board's records. Companies whose base we couldn't confirm are left out too, for now. If we left your company out by mistake, use the form below and we'll add it.

What a listing shows. Name, phone, base city, a website we checked (it loaded and showed the same phone number), and the LSLBC license and class with the year LSLBC first issued it, when the company matches LSLBC's records by exact phone or exact name. A missing badge only means we couldn't match a company to those records with certainty. Ask the company for its number and check it.

Order. Alphabetical. Nobody pays to be listed, and whether a company works with autoants never changes its order or how it looks.

What we never show. Star ratings or reviews, and anything that isn't a public fact about the company.
