How to hire: the Louisiana rules

What license should a tree service in Louisiana have?

An arborist license from the Louisiana Department of Agriculture and Forestry's Horticulture Commission. State law requires it of anyone who advertises as or works as an arborist, which covers removing a tree or part of one and pruning it. A company meets the rule with a licensed arborist on staff, or one directly supervising the job.

Source: La. R.S. 3:3808(A) · La. R.S. 3:3804(C)

How do I check a tree company's arborist license?

LDAF posts its list of licensed arborists as a download on its arborist page. Look for the company, or the person who will run your job. A truck with the company's sign on it should also carry the letters AR and the last four digits of the license, at least 2 inches high, on both sides.

Source: LDAF arborist page · LAC 7:XXIX.115(D)

What should a tree service put in writing?

The Horticulture Commission's rules call for a written contract that states the services and the price. Make sure it names which trees and limbs, whether the stump gets ground, and whether the debris is hauled away. Those three are where quotes quietly differ.

Source: LAC 7:XXIX.117(E)(4)

Does a Louisiana tree service need insurance?

An arborist has to show proof of liability insurance to get and renew the state license. Ask for a certificate sent to you by the insurance company, not a photo of a card, and ask about workers' compensation if a crew will be on your property.

Source: La. R.S. 3:3808(A)(2)(b) · LAC 7:XXIX.117(E)(2)

Should a tree be topped?

The International Society of Arboriculture calls topping an unacceptable pruning technique: it weakens the tree and invites decay. A company that offers to top a healthy tree is telling you something about its work.

Source: ISA, Why Topping Hurts Trees

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. Who holds the LDAF arborist license, and will that person run or supervise this job?
  2. Can your insurance company send me a certificate of liability insurance?
  3. Is stump grinding included? Is the debris hauled away?
  4. Does this job need a permit from my city or parish, and who gets it?
  5. Will I get a written contract with the work and the price?

What the work usually covers

Tree removal, pruning, stump grinding, debris haul-off, storm cleanup and tree risk assessments. Ask before assuming any one of them is in the price.
