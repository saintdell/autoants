How to choose an HVAC company

  1. Check the state license yourself. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors (LSLBC) licenses this work: heating, air conditioning, ventilation, duct work and refrigeration is a subclassification of its Mechanical license, and the board says a license is required when the job's value exceeds $10,000 (LSLBC). Ask for the license number and look it up on the board's own search, by number or by company name. The badges in this guide link to the board's record.
  2. Ask whether the technician is EPA Section 608 certified. Federal rules require technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerants to be certified, by passing an EPA-approved test (EPA). It's the person who holds it, so ask about the technician coming to your home.
  3. Ask about the warranty, and get it in writing. The equipment maker's warranty and the company's own warranty on its work are two different things; ask how long each lasts and what it covers.
  4. Ask about financing before you need it. If a replacement will be financed, ask who the lender is and what the rate and term are, and compare them with your own bank or credit union.
  5. Ask about after-hours service. Do they answer nights and weekends, and does an after-hours visit cost more? In a New Orleans August that answer matters.
  6. For a replacement, get more than one written quote. Compare the same things on each: the equipment and its size, what work is included, removal of the old unit, and the warranty.

How this list was built

Who's listed. A company is listed when it is based in Orleans or Jefferson Parish, has a phone, and either holds an active LSLBC license in the heating and air-conditioning subclassification, or appears in public business listings under an HVAC, air-conditioning or heating category. Duplicates (same phone) are listed once. We also need to confirm a real local base: an LSLBC license match, or a street address in the parish shown on the company's own website next to the same phone, or on a City of New Orleans occupational license.

Who's left out. Companies holding only the broader Mechanical license that we found in no HVAC listing (that license also covers plumbing and piping work); a license held under a person's own name; retail stores, parts suppliers and car-repair shops; companies whose main trade is something else (hood cleaning, renovation, machine shops, building controls); and listings that look like call-routing services rather than a local company: an out-of-state phone number, three or more different names on near-identical phone numbers, a throwaway ".site" web address, or a name made of a city plus a service ("Kenner HVAC Repair"), unless it matches an LSLBC license. Companies whose base we couldn't confirm are left out too, for now. If we left your company out by mistake, use the form below and we'll add it.

What a listing shows. Name, phone, base city, a website we checked (it loaded and showed the same phone number), and the LSLBC license with the year LSLBC first issued it, when the company matches LSLBC's records by exact phone or exact name. A missing badge only means we couldn't match a company to those records with certainty. Ask the company for its number and check it.

Order. Alphabetical. Nobody pays to be listed, and whether a company works with autoants never changes its order or how it looks.

What we never show. Star ratings or reviews, and anything that isn't a public fact about the company.
