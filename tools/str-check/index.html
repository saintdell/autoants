Can this New Orleans address have a short-term rental?

A free check for one address: the zoning district, whether its City square already has a residential STR licence, and open code cases, from the City's own records. Then the questions only you can answer.

Published 2026-10-20 · Updated 2026-10-20 · Rules read from City pages 2026-10-20

Can I get a short-term rental permit for this address?

It depends. This check reads three public facts: the zoning district must allow a Non-Commercial STR, the City square must not already have one (one per square, counting all four sides), and the address shouldn't have open code cases. It also asks about the operator, who must live on the lot during guest stays. The City checks more than this tool can: ownership, taxes and liens, training and documents. Whole-unit Commercial STRs are a separate permit and now need conditional-use approval.

How we match "the block"

The City counts one Non-Commercial STR per city block, meaning the whole square: every lot facing any of its four sides plus interior lots (CZO §21.8.C.18(m)). We find the address on the City's address-point layer, take the square that contains it from the City's square layer, then count active Non-Commercial licences from the City's licence list (data.nola.gov ufdg-ajws) whose address is one of the square's own address points, or whose map point falls inside the square. Matching by address catches licences the City mapped in the wrong place. Operator permits and exempt lodging don't count. We also read the City's own NSTR map, which marks squares that already have an NSTR or a bed and breakfast; when it disagrees with our count, the answer is "needs review" and the City's map decides.

The rule is in force: the Fifth Circuit upheld it on Aug. 5, 2026 (Bodin v. City of New Orleans, No. 25-30524).

Questions

Do I need a homestead exemption?

Not any more. The 2019 homestead rule was struck down in 2022. The current rule is an operator who lives on the lot during guest stays.

What counts as a block?

The whole City square: every lot facing any of its four sides plus interior lots (CZO §21.8.C.18(m)). Not just your side of the street.

Is the one-per-block rule still in force?

Yes. The Fifth Circuit upheld it on Aug. 5, 2026 (Bodin v. City of New Orleans).

Who decides?

The City's STR Administration. This check is an estimate from public records. Help desk (504) 658-7144, str@nola.gov.
