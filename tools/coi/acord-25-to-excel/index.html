Three kinds of ACORD 25 PDF

  • Fillable. The agent's system filled the form's fields. These are read field by field, the surest kind.
  • Flattened. Printed to PDF, so the fields are gone but the text is still there. Read by position, using the form's labels.
  • Scanned. A picture of the page. Read with OCR, and every row is marked for a check.

How each certificate is read

  1. Form fields first. A fillable ACORD 25 carries every value in named fields; those are read exactly, with no guessing.
  2. Layout next. A flattened PDF still has text. Each value is placed by the form's own printed labels (POLICY NUMBER, POLICY EXP, LIMITS, INSURER A).
  3. OCR for scans. A scan or phone photo is read by text recognition and placed the same way. Every OCR value is marked for a human check.
  4. A local model, last. Only for a value still missing, and only kept if the exact text is on the certificate. Also marked for a check.

Each row has a confidence from 0 to 1. Anything under 0.8, any missing policy number or expiry date, and any expiry that isn't after the effective date is marked needs_check and highlighted in the spreadsheet.

Facts only

Facts as printed on each certificate. A certificate is issued as a matter of information only and confers no rights; it doesn't show whether coverage fits your contract. For that, ask the issuing agent or your own insurance adviser. The converter reports what the certificate prints and when the dates fall. It doesn't judge whether coverage fits a contract, a lease or a law.

How to convert ACORD 25 certificates to Excel

  1. Save the certificates as PDFs. Keep the original PDF from the agent when you can; it's often fillable.
  2. Upload. Add the PDFs and convert.
  3. Open the .xlsx. One row per policy. Sort or filter by expiration, insurer or insured.
  4. Check yellow rows. Compare each needs_check row with its certificate before relying on it.

Questions

Why is one certificate several rows?

One certificate can list four or more policies (general liability, auto, umbrella, workers comp), each with its own number, insurer and dates. One row per policy keeps dates and limits sortable.

Can I import the CSV into my own tracker?

Yes. The CSV has a header row and plain values: ISO dates (YYYY-MM-DD) and limits as whole dollars with no commas.

Does it read the description of operations box?

Yes, as text, into description_of_operations. It's copied as printed; the converter doesn't interpret it.

Will it tell me whether a sub carries enough insurance?

No. It copies what the certificate prints: the limits, the dates, the boxes marked. Comparing those with your contract's terms is your call, or a question for the issuing agent. A certificate confers no rights by itself.

What happens to my files?

They're read in memory and a temporary folder that's deleted as soon as the spreadsheet is made. The spreadsheet and calendar download for 72 hours, then they're deleted too. We keep the order record, not the certificates.
