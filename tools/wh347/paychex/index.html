What the converter reads from Paychex

The Payroll Journal is built for a printed page: totals rows, subtotals and a title block. Delete the title rows and any company total rows so it's one header row and one row per worker. It doesn't carry labor classifications; put those in the workers sheet or a Labor Class column.

How to export from Paychex and convert to WH-347

  1. Open reports. In Paychex Flex, open Analytics & Reports (or Quick Reports on the dashboard), then View All Reports.
  2. Pick the Payroll Journal. Choose Payroll Journal, then Create Report.
  3. Set the week. Choose the date range for the workweek, all employees, with employee totals.
  4. Download. Download the report as Excel.
  5. Hours per day. Run the Timecards report with the Daily option for the same dates, if you use Paychex time and attendance, and export it.
  6. Upload. Upload the journal, the time report and your workers sheet, enter the WD number and modification, and convert.

Menu names are from the provider's help pages as of October 2026 and change over time. If yours differ, look for the report by name.

Questions

The Payroll Journal has a block per employee, not a row. What do I do?

Choose the option for employee totals when you create the report, so each employee has one totals line, and delete the detail lines. Or use the generic CSV template on this site and paste the numbers in.

Paychex shows OASDI. Is that FICA?

Yes. OASDI is the Social Security part of FICA; the converter adds OASDI and Medicare into the FICA column of the WH-347.

Is this connected to my Paychex account?

No. Nothing logs in to Paychex. You export the report and upload it; the file is converted and deleted.

Does the converter sign or submit the payroll?

No. It makes a draft for your review. The contractor checks the Statement of Compliance boxes, signs it and submits the payroll (a sub sends it to the prime). The signature has to be handwritten or a legally valid electronic signature.

What does the rate check compare?

Each worker's cash rate against the WD basic rate, and rate plus fringe credit plus cash in lieu against the WD rate plus fringe for that classification. It also prices overtime hours paid short and hours over 40 paid at straight time, and flags apprentices without registration details. It checks the numbers you gave it against the WD you named; it doesn't decide whether the contract carries Davis-Bacon or which classification a worker actually performed.
