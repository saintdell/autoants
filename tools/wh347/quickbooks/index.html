What the converter reads from QuickBooks

QuickBooks writes names as 'Last, First M', which the converter splits for you. Its payroll reports don't carry the labor classification or hours per day. If your export is laid out with employees as columns rather than rows, switch the report's layout (or transpose it in Excel) so each employee is one row.

How to export from QuickBooks and convert to WH-347

  1. Open the payroll reports. In QuickBooks Online, go to Reports, then Standard reports, and scroll to the Payroll section.
  2. Pick a per-employee report. Open Payroll Summary by Employee (or Payroll Details, if your account lists it): one row or block per employee with hours, pay and taxes.
  3. Set the week. Set the date range to the workweek you're reporting and select Apply.
  4. Export. Select Export (the share or export icon), then Export to Excel.
  5. Hours per day. Run Time Activities by Employee Detail for the same dates (or, in QuickBooks Time, the Hours by Date report) and export it: that's the time report.
  6. Upload. Upload the export, the time report and your workers sheet, enter the WD number and modification, and convert.

Menu names are from the provider's help pages as of October 2026 and change over time. If yours differ, look for the report by name.

Questions

QuickBooks lists employees as columns. Can the converter read that?

Not as-is: it needs one row per worker. In Excel, copy the table and Paste Special with Transpose, keep the header row on top, and save as .xlsx or .csv.

Can I use QuickBooks Time instead of QuickBooks Online time tracking?

Yes. Export QuickBooks Time's Hours by Date (or Timesheet Log) for the week as CSV and upload it as the time report. It needs the worker's name, the date and the hours.

My export includes the SSN column. Is that a problem?

It's cut to the last 4 digits before the file is read, and the draft shows only the last 4. Better: leave that column out of the export, or use the SSN (last 4) column.

Does the converter sign or submit the payroll?

No. It makes a draft for your review. The contractor checks the Statement of Compliance boxes, signs it and submits the payroll (a sub sends it to the prime). The signature has to be handwritten or a legally valid electronic signature.

What does the rate check compare?

Each worker's cash rate against the WD basic rate, and rate plus fringe credit plus cash in lieu against the WD rate plus fringe for that classification. It also prices overtime hours paid short and hours over 40 paid at straight time, and flags apprentices without registration details. It checks the numbers you gave it against the WD you named; it doesn't decide whether the contract carries Davis-Bacon or which classification a worker actually performed.
