How does an HVAC company stop missing calls in the July rush?

Make sure every call gets an answer even when nobody can pick up: a text back from your business within seconds, an AI that answers and texts you the job, or both. In New Orleans the normal July high is 91°F with about 23 days at 90°F or hotter, so a caller with no cool air has every reason to try the next shop.

What the city's records show, and what they don't

Orleans Parish issued 2,022 HVAC permits from October 2025 to September 2026. The busiest month was May (249) and the slowest was January (121); July had 196. But permits track installs and replacements, not repair calls, so no public record counts the summer repair rush. Your own phone does.

Three ways to cover the calls you miss

  1. Text back every missed call. The caller gets a text from your business within seconds, naming your shop, with STOP to opt out. It never texts a call you answered, and never texts the same caller twice inside 12 hours. A landline can't receive it.
  2. Let an AI answer. It says it's an AI in its first sentence, takes the details, texts you the job and passes a real emergency to a person by rules you write. It never quotes a price, diagnoses a system or promises an arrival time.
  3. Have a person answer. An answering service or someone on staff. That's the better choice when callers need a person every time. See AI receptionist, answering service or a dispatcher.

Most shops start with the first. The second covers nights and weekends.

Set it up in spring, not in July

  1. Register for business texting. Carriers block texts from unregistered business numbers, and the review usually takes two to three weeks. Your shop gets its own registration under your legal name. (How we handle texts and calls)
  2. Send tune-up reminders to past customers who agreed to hear from you, so tune-ups get booked in spring, before the heat, while your crew has room.
  3. Put a way to book on the first phone screen, so a customer who can't get through can still ask for a time. 16 of 45 local HVAC sites didn't have one.
  4. Make your Google hours honest. Only list late hours if someone, or an AI on a line you approved, really answers then. A text-back alone doesn't make the shop open.
