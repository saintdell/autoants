AI receptionist, answering service or a dispatcher: which fits an HVAC company?

If you mostly lose calls while you're on a job or after hours, a missed-call text or an AI receptionist covers those calls without adding a person. If your callers need a person who can make judgment calls, an answering service or a dispatcher on staff is the better choice.

How to decide, step by step

  1. Count a week of missed calls, and when they happen. On a job, at lunch, after 5, on weekends. We do this with you before we start.
  2. Mostly during the workday, while techs are busy? Start with the missed-call text. It's the smallest step.
  3. Many after hours, and some are real emergencies? AI answering with an emergency hand-off to a person, or a live answering service.
  4. You also need someone scheduling techs all day? That's a dispatcher. The text-back or the AI can still cover nights and weekends.
  5. Callers who won't talk to an AI? Some won't, and that's their call. A person is the better fit for them.

Questions

Will callers know it's an AI?

Yes. It says it's an AI in its first sentence, and again if anyone asks.

Can the AI book a tech?

It takes the job details and texts them to you. You text back to approve before the customer hears yes.

What does the AI never do?

It never quotes a price, diagnoses a system, promises an arrival time or takes a card number. Those calls go to you.

Can I keep my answering service?

Yes. Some shops keep a live service for calls and use autoants for the text-back, quote follow-ups and booking.
