How to get a New Orleans business on Apple Maps, Bing and Yelp

Set up four free accounts: Apple Business (for Apple Maps and Siri), Bing Places, Bing Webmaster Tools and Yelp. Each one checks that you're real in its own way: two proofs of the company for Apple, a PIN for Bing, a phone call for Yelp. Plan on an afternoon of typing and one to two weeks of waiting. Skip the paid extras until you've decided on them.

Before you start: write one sheet of facts

Every listing should say exactly the same thing. A different phone number or an extra “LLC” in one place makes it harder for Google, Bing and AI assistants to tell which business is yours. Write these once and paste from the sheet:

  • Name as it's on your sign and truck. No added keywords: Bing says it doesn't accept them in a business name.
  • Legal name and filing address, only for the forms that ask (Apple's organization record does).
  • Phone, website, email, hours, and the category that fits best.
  • Service area: the towns you really drive to.
  • A short and a long description, plain and true.
  • Real photos: your logo, finished jobs, your vehicle. For Yelp, a clear photo of the owner's face.
  • Your EIN (or D-U-N-S number) and a login for your domain's DNS settings, for Apple.

Do Google first. If your Google Business Profile is already verified, Bing can import it, and Bing Webmaster Tools can import your site from Google Search Console. That saves two verification steps.

1. Apple Business: Apple Maps and Siri

Apple Business is Apple's free service for businesses. Apple says the information you put there reaches people across Apple Maps, Mail, Wallet, Siri and more. It opened on April 14, 2026, and the old Apple Business Connect address now sends you there.

  1. Sign up at business.apple.com. A personal Apple Account can manage brands and locations only; Apple's “Create an Apple Account for Work” option opens the rest. We made a work account with our business email.
  2. Enter your organization: the legal name and the address on your filing.
  3. Verify the organization with two methods: a Business ID (D-U-N-S Number or EIN), a TXT record on your domain's DNS, or an official document such as a business license or utility bill. Apple says this can take up to five business days. If it isn't approved within 60 days of signing up, Apple deletes the organization and its accounts, so don't let it sit.
  4. Add your location (Brands, then Locations, then Add Location): the name, the address and map pin, the primary category, the phone number, hours and website.
  5. Working from home? Apple's help pages don't describe a service-area or hide-address setting. We're adding our own Maps place next and will update this page with what we find.

Skip: ads on Apple Maps. Apple now sells ads on Maps in the US and Canada, with a $150 sign-up credit for new advertisers and a credit card required. If you're a trade, you can't run them anyway: Apple's Maps ad policy bans ads that promote home services, including plumbing, electrical, locksmith, HVAC, pest control, roofing and general contracting.

2. Bing Places: Bing's map and local results

Bing Places is a free Bing service for claiming or adding your listing. Microsoft says Copilot, with web search on, pulls information from the Bing search service to build its answers.

  1. Search Bing for your business first. If a listing exists and isn't claimed, claim it. If not, create one.
  2. Fastest route: import your Google profile. Choose Import from your Google Business Profile and how often to sync (weekly, every two weeks or monthly). If the Google profile is already verified, Bing verifies the import automatically. Watch for duplicates, and note that a synced listing is edited in Google, then synced.
  3. Otherwise, verify with a PIN. Bing sends a six-digit PIN by phone, text, email or postcard, and not every business is offered every option. Phone is offered only if the listing already has your business number, unedited. Bing says a postcard typically arrives in 5 to 6 days and can take up to 12 business days. Ours is in the mail.
  4. Working from home? In Basic details, answer “Are customers expected to visit your address?” and choose to show only your city and ZIP code. Bing still keeps the real address for verification, won't take a PO box, and lets you list up to 20 service areas.
  5. Wait for publishing. Bing says a verified listing usually takes 7 to 12 calendar days to go live, and that frequent edits while it processes can slow it down.

3. Bing Webmaster Tools: your website in Bing and Copilot

This one is about your website, not your listing. It's where Bing tells you how it sees your pages, and, since February 2026, how often its AI answers cite them.

  1. Sign in at bing.com/webmasters.
  2. Import from Google Search Console if your site is verified there. Bing adds and verifies the site automatically and brings your sitemaps along. That's what we did. Otherwise add the site by hand and verify it with an XML file, a meta tag or a DNS record.
  3. Check that your sitemap is listed. Bing says data usually takes about 48 hours to appear.
  4. Open the AI Performance report. It counts how often your pages are cited in Microsoft Copilot, Bing's AI-generated summaries and some partner integrations, page by page, with the searches the AI used.

4. Yelp

Many owners treat Yelp as a dead page. ChatGPT still reads it: in a BrightLocal study published in August 2026, Yelp showed up as a source in 80% of ChatGPT's answers to local searches.

  1. Claim your page by searching for your business at biz.yelp.com, or add it if it isn't there. Yelp walks you through verifying by phone. If phone verification fails, Yelp has you send a report to its Support team.
  2. Add the owner photo. Yelp wants a real, clear photo of you. It rejects logos, storefronts, group shots and stock photos, and a moderator has to approve the photo before you can reply to reviews.
  3. Working from home? In Business Information, edit the Service Area section and say yes to serving customers at their location. Add up to 6 major cities within a 50-mile radius. If your categories qualify, the street address stays hidden. Ours shows only “New Orleans” and the ZIP code.
  4. Fill in the rest: categories, services, hours, photos and the description, from your sheet.
  5. Don't ask customers for Yelp reviews. Yelp's own guidelines say businesses should never ask customers to write reviews. Ask for Google reviews, the same way for every customer.

Skip, or at least decide on purpose: Yelp's Upgrade Package starts with a 14-day free trial and then bills monthly; it adds a logo, a photo slideshow, a call-to-action button and takes competitors' ads off your page. Yelp Ads were offered to us too; Yelp says self-serve ads have no term contract and can be canceled any time. Neither is needed to be listed. Before accepting any free trial, put the date it starts billing on your calendar.

If you work from home: the address rules in one place

  • Google: if you don't serve customers at your address, remove it from your Business Profile. List up to 20 service areas by city or ZIP code, not a radius, within about 2 hours' drive of your base.
  • Bing Places: answer no to the visit question; show only city and ZIP; up to 20 service areas; no PO boxes.
  • Yelp: a service area of up to 6 cities hides the street, if your categories allow it.
  • Apple: not described in Apple's help as of October 30, 2026.

Whichever platform, verify with your real address, then open the live listing after it publishes and check what it shows.

Questions

Do I have to pay to be on Apple Maps, Bing or Yelp?

No. Apple Business and Bing Places are free, and a Yelp business page comes with free tools. Each one offers paid extras on top: ads on Apple Maps, Yelp's Upgrade Package and Yelp Ads. None of them is needed to be listed.

How long does it take?

The typing took us most of an afternoon for all four. The waiting is longer: Apple says organization verification can take up to five business days, a Bing postcard PIN typically takes 5 to 6 days and up to 12 business days, and Bing says a verified listing usually takes 7 to 12 calendar days to publish.

Can I keep my home address off these listings?

If customers don't come to your address, yes on Google, Bing and Yelp. Google says to remove the address from your Business Profile, Bing lets you show only your city and ZIP code, and Yelp hides the street when you set a service area, if your categories allow it. Apple's help pages don't describe a hide-address setting.

Will this get my business named by Siri, Copilot or ChatGPT?

Nobody can promise that. Apple says Apple Business information reaches people across Apple Maps, Siri, Wallet and Mail, and Microsoft says Copilot pulls web results from Bing. Correct listings give those assistants the right facts to read.
