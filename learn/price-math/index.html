Margin dollars, not margin percent.

Your bank account doesn't pay you in percent. It pays you in the dollars left after each job covers its materials and labor, times the number of jobs. Put in your own numbers and see what a price cut, a faster quote or a caught call does to those dollars.

How the math works

Three lines of arithmetic.

No industry averages, no outside statistics. Just your numbers run through these.

Margin dollars pay the bills

jobs × average job × gross margin. That's what's left each month for overhead and profit after every job pays for its own materials and labor.

A discount comes out of the margin

Cut the price 5% and your cost per job doesn't move, so the whole 5% comes off your margin. On a 45% margin you keep 40 cents where you kept 45, and you need 45 ÷ 40 = 1.125 times the jobs to stand still.

Break-even is a job count

overhead ÷ margin per job. Every job past that line is profit. A raise lowers the line; a cut raises it.
