Setup guide

Send us job photos

Time: ~2 minYou stay the owner

Your weekly posts and short videos come from your real jobs. Here’s how to get the photos to us, and what to keep out of them.

Time is per job, once you’re in the habit.

Every job, in order

  1. Ask the homeowner first. Something like: “Mind if we take a few photos of the work for our page? No house number, no faces.” Get their OK in a text or email. No OK, we don’t use that job.
  2. Shoot the before before the crew touches anything. Note where you stood.
  3. Shoot a few during the work: the problem up close, the steps.
  4. Shoot the after from the same spot, same height, same zoom, pointing the same way. Hold the phone upright if you can.
  5. Send them to the shared album we set up with you on the kickoff call, or text them to the number in our welcome email. Add one line: the job, the neighborhood or town (never the street address), and the homeowner’s OK (a screenshot of their text is fine).
You’ll seeWithin a couple of days, the post or video made from that job, sent to you to approve. Nothing goes up until you say yes.

The photo rules

  • No faces unless that person said yes. Easiest: wait for the homeowner and the neighbors to be out of the frame.
  • No house numbers, street signs or license plates. Shoot wide enough to show the work and tight enough to leave those out. Watch the mailbox and the car in the driveway.
  • Ask before you shoot the house. The whole house or yard goes up only with the homeowner’s OK.
  • No names on mailboxes, trucks, papers or screens. Leave the insurance paperwork out too.
  • Inside a home: the equipment and the finished work only. No mail, screens or personal things.
  • No other companies’ signs or trucks in the shot.
  • Only your crew’s photos of your own jobs. Nothing from the internet.

If something slips into a photo, we blur it or drop the photo. We never say where the job was beyond the neighborhood or town.

Before and after that look right

  • Same spot, same height, same zoom. A matched pair looks like one job. A mismatched pair looks like two.
  • Daylight, and the sun behind you.
  • Show the change: the whole roof, the whole unit, the whole drain line. Then one close-up of the detail.
  • The same before and after twice doesn’t count. A before/after with no change in it says something untrue about the work.
