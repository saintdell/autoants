Setup guide

Approve your postcards

Time: ~5 minYou stay the owner

Nothing mails until you’ve said yes to how the card reads and who it goes to.

The cards we might send for you

  • Past customers: when their service is due again or on their job’s anniversary, from your own job list.
  • Your trade’s permit card: homes where a related permit was just pulled, from public city records.
  • The neighbors: the homes around a job you just finished.

Approve the proof

  1. We email you a proof of the front and back, stamped PROOF.
  2. Check your business name: it must match your license exactly, with your license number.
  3. Check your phone number, your booking link, the offer and its end date, your photo and your return address. Scan the QR code: it should open your booking page.
  4. Reply YES, or tell us what to change. We write down the day you said yes. If a word changes later, it needs your yes again.

Approve each mailing

Before each mailing you see how many cards, which areas, and what the postage comes to. Nothing goes out without your yes.

  • Past-customer and neighbor cards print and mail through your own PostGrid account, on your card. You see that bill directly.
  • Your trade’s permit card is $1.30 a card, mailed.
You’ll seeOur note that the batch went out, with the count. Each card has its own code, so calls and scans from it show up on your monthly results page.

Who’s on the mailing list

  • Past customers: from your own export. One card per house. A house we mailed in the last 60 days is skipped, and so is anyone whose last job was more than three years ago.
  • Permit cards: public permit records only, addressed to the owner “or current resident.” One card per house. Never moving lists, never real-estate listing data.
  • Neighbors: about 25 nearby homes, never the customer’s own house. With the customer’s OK the card can say “on Mulberry Dr”: the street, never the house number.

The do-not-mail list

  • Anyone who asks you not to send mail: text or email us the address, any time.
  • Once it’s on the list, that address is never mailed again.
  • The list stays even if you leave us, so people who said stop stay stopped.

What we never put on a card

A checker reads every card before it can print. It refuses:

  • Insurance-claim talk“We work with your insurance,” “claims specialist,” “adjuster.” Louisiana law bars contractors from advertising claim help.
  • Deductible offers“We pay your deductible,” “no out-of-pocket.” Also against Louisiana law.
  • Words that need proofBest, #1, guaranteed, cheapest, lowest price, risk-free.
  • Fake rush“Act now,” “only 3 spots left,” unless there’s a real deadline we can point to.
  • Anything that looks officialNothing that looks like a city or government notice.
  • A name that isn’t on your licenseYour exact licensed name and number only. “Licensed” or “insured” only when it’s true and on file.
  • Anyone’s private detailsNo customer names, house numbers or faces.
  • Other kinds of offersNo real-estate, legal or notary offers.

Door hangers follow the same rules. Your crew hangs them on door handles only, never in a mailbox: federal law keeps mailboxes for the mail.
