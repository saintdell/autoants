What it never does

What each assistant will never do.

Each assistant has one job. Here's the job, and the things it's built never to do.

You approve before any customer hears yes.

Nothing goes to your customers in a new form until you've approved the wording. When the receptionist takes a job, you text back to approve it before the customer hears yes.

Each assistant asks you before every send until you've approved two weeks of its drafts without changing a word. One change restarts the count for that kind of message.

The off switch is yours.

If anything looks wrong, turn off call forwarding yourself. I give you the code on day one and write it on your page. Then call me. You can change or pause any assistant at any time.

If one gets something wrong, I tell you first, in the weekly update, whether you noticed or not.

None of them:

  • negotiates a price or gives out an address;
  • spends money or opens accounts; tools run on your account and card;
  • texts anyone who didn't contact you first or say yes.

One by one

  • Text-BackTexts a missed call back once, naming your business, with STOP.Never: texts a call you answered, or texts the same caller again inside 12 hours.
  • RepliesEach customer reply comes to you, tagged by assistant.Never: answers a customer itself (only the STOP confirmation and your approved help line), or replies to a stranger.
  • ResponderDrafts a fast reply to a new lead.Never: writes to anyone who didn't contact you.
  • Comment-to-QuoteOne message, labeled automatic, with your quote link.Never: messages people who didn't comment, sends a drip, or names a price.
  • SchedulerOffers three open times from your calendar.Never: books or writes to your calendar by itself. If your calendar feed is down, it says “call for times.”
  • Quote assistantPrices from your own price book and drafts the proposal.Never: lets an AI set a price, shows your markup, or prices a job that needs a site visit.
  • Quote follow-upNudges a quote that went quiet. A reply stops it.Never: sends without your OK.
  • RemindersAppointment reminders, and reminders to past customers.Never: texts anyone without a consent record.
  • ReviewerThe same review ask to every customer.Never: picks who gets asked, or pays for reviews.
  • ReceptionistAnswers calls you can't, says it's an AI, takes the job details, and passes a real emergency to a person.Never: quotes a price, promises an arrival time, diagnoses, takes a card number, or dials out.
  • Invoice follow-upDrafts reminders for overdue invoices.Never: touches money.
  • COI GuardChecks your subs' insurance certificates monthly.Never: fills in a field it can't read.
  • Call auditFlags missed, slow and unbooked calls.Never: changes anything itself.
  • TrackerMatches calls, scans and forms to your jobs.Never: records a call without telling the caller (recording is off by default), counts a job it can't trace, or estimates a number.
  • Neighbor HangerHangers for the homes around a finished job.Never: prints a customer's address without their OK, prints stars you didn't supply, uses an unapproved design, or goes in mailboxes.
  • Anniversary MailerReminds past customers when the service is due again.Never: mails anyone who opted out, mails a house twice inside 60 days, or uses wording you didn't approve.
  • Referral ExchangeAsks a homeowner once if they'd like a quote from a trusted client in the next trade.Never: shares anything without the homeowner's recorded yes, asks twice, or names a partner you don't trust.
  • Weather TriggerReadies a campaign when the Weather Service issues an alert.Never: posts or sends by itself, uses fear or fake urgency, or calls a watch a warning.
