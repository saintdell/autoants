How your AC's outdoor unit works, and what a tech checks first

The outdoor half of a central AC carries the heat out of your house. Here it is with the panels off.

Tap Call for cooling, then Capacitor fails. Watch the fan.

The fan stopped, but the thermostat is still calling? A common cause is a failed capacitor, and it's the first thing a tech checks. Don't open the unit: a capacitor can hold a charge even with the power off. Call a pro.

Illustration of a generic unit, simplified; not a repair guide. Figure made with Colorfigures.

What's working when it cools?

The thermostat calls for cooling. A switch in the outdoor unit (the contactor) pulls in, the capacitor helps the fan motor and the compressor start and keep running, and the fan pulls outside air through the coil and blows the house's heat out the top.

What can I check myself?

  • The air filter. If it's clogged, put in a clean one of the same size.
  • The breakers. If the AC's breaker has tripped (handle in the middle), push it to OFF, then ON, once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a tech.

Leave the panels closed.

What does a tech check first?

That the thermostat's call reaches the unit, then the contactor, the capacitor, the fan motor and the compressor. Then the coil and the refrigerant.
