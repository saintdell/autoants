How your gas water heater works, and what a tech checks first

A gas tank water heater with a standing pilot, the burner cover off. Many newer heaters light differently; the idea is the same.

Tap Use hot water, then Pilot goes out.

No hot water, and the pilot is out? With no flame on the thermocouple, the gas valve stays shut: that's the safety doing its job. Call a pro. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas company from outside.

Illustration of a generic unit, simplified; not a repair guide. Figure made with Colorfigures.

What's working when it heats?

A small pilot flame burns all the time. Its heat on the thermocouple tells the gas valve a flame is there. When the water cools, the valve opens gas to the burner, the pilot lights it, and the hot gases rise through the flue in the middle of the tank, heating the water, and out the vent.

What can I check myself?

Nothing inside the burner area. Keep the space around the heater clear. If you ever smell gas, don't switch anything on or off: leave and call the gas company from outside.

What does a tech check first?

Why the pilot went out: the pilot and the thermocouple, the gas supply, then the gas valve, the burner and the vent.
