How ISN reports are laid out

ISN (Inspection Support Network) is the scheduling and delivery system many inspection companies use. The report you receive through it may come from ISN's own report writer or from another program, so layouts vary. Many list each finding with labeled fields such as item, observation, location, recommendation and a severity or category.

How we read them

  • Labeled findings are read field by field: the observation and the inspector's recommendation together make the quoted item.
  • If your report uses a layout we don't recognize, a general reader picks out paragraphs that carry the inspector's action words, and marks any item read with lower confidence.
  • Findings rated acceptable or not inspected are left off.

What you get

  1. Every item in your inspector's words. We quote the report word for word. We don't reword it, shorten it or add our own opinion.
  2. Grouped by trade. Roofing, plumbing, electrical, heating and air, structural and foundation, pest and termite, general handyman and more, so each contractor gets the items they do.
  3. Safety items first. Items your inspector rated or described as safety items come first, then repair items, then monitor and maintenance items. The rating shown is always the inspector's.
  4. What to ask each contractor. License, insurance, a written price item by item, permits, timing, plus a few trade-specific questions.
  5. A printable PDF and a checklist you can share with your agent, family or contractors.
